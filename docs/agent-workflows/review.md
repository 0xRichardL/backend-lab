# Reviewing evidence and findings

Use this guidance to review an experiment, write findings, or compare frameworks. Preserve the learner's notes and opinions; propose corrections with evidence rather than silently rewriting them.

## Review priorities

Check, in order: whether the experiment answers its question; whether behavior is correct and reproducible; whether conclusions have appropriate evidence and scope; whether implementation is idiomatic; whether another reader can run it from its README; whether it is isolated; and whether important current-scope edge cases are covered. Treat missing evidence, unfair comparisons, incorrect behavior, leaked secrets, and irreproducible instructions as substantive. Treat formatting and personal style as minor unless they obscure the lesson.

## Write findings

Distinguish these labels where ambiguity matters:

- **Fact:** Supported by documentation, source code, or a reproducible result.
- **Observation:** Experienced directly in this experiment.
- **Inference:** Derived from facts or observations.
- **Opinion:** A preference influenced by the learner's goals or experience.

Tie strengths and limitations to the task, version, and scenario. Explain what was easier or harder and why. Mark conclusions provisional when evidence is narrow. Do not promote framework marketing or unverified tutorial claims into findings.

## Compare fairly

Compare implementations that meet the same scenario and constraints. Use each framework's normal idioms rather than forcing identical internal architecture; record meaningful deviations. Separate language trade-offs from framework trade-offs. Do not rank untested capabilities or make performance claims without comparable measurements.
